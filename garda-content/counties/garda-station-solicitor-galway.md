If you have been arrested anywhere in Galway, or asked to attend a station for interview, you are entitled to legal advice before you are questioned. Ring 087 122 3080 and we will tell you honestly what we can do and how fast.

## Stations in the city

Mill Street is the main city station and handles most custody work for Galway city, with Salthill covering the west of the city. Galway has a young population and a busy night time economy, so weekend arrests for public order and road traffic matters are common, and they still carry consequences worth taking advice on.

## Stations across the county

Galway is the largest county in Connacht and the terrain drives everything about timing.

East of the city, the principal stations are Tuam, Ballinasloe, Loughrea, Athenry, Gort and Portumna. West of the city, in Connemara, they include Oughterard, Clifden, Moycullen, Carraroe and Carna. Clifden is more than an hour beyond Galway city itself.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Give your name, address and date of birth, then stop. Say that you want a solicitor before any interview and ask the member in charge to ring us on your behalf. Ring 087 122 3080.

## Where Galway cases are heard

District Court business for the city is heard at Galway Courthouse, which is also where the Circuit Criminal Court sits for the county. District Court sittings are held around the county in towns including Tuam, Ballinasloe, Loughrea, Gort and Clifden.

Parts of Galway are in the Gaeltacht. A person may seek to have proceedings conducted in Irish, and if that matters to you it is worth raising at the outset rather than on the morning of a hearing, because it affects how a case is listed.

## Being straight with you about attendance

Ferrys Solicitors LLP has five offices and all five are in Dublin. There is no Galway office and we will not dress one up.

Galway city is about 210 kilometres from Dublin and the M6 runs the whole way, so it is around two to two and a half hours by car in normal conditions. Ballinasloe and Loughrea are closer still. Connemara is a different matter: Clifden or Carna can be three and a half to four hours from us, and in poor weather longer.

Because six criminal defence solicitors work a rota rather than a single mobile being answered by one person, you can normally get a solicitor on the phone quickly whatever the hour, and telephone advice can happen well before anyone travels. On attendance in person, we will give you a realistic time on the call. If we cannot get there before you are interviewed, we will say so and help you find someone who can.

## What to do while you wait

1. Ask the member in charge to note that you have requested a solicitor.
2. Ask for telephone advice now rather than waiting on an arrival.
3. Say nothing about the allegation to anyone in the station.
4. Do not sign a statement or memo of interview before you have taken advice.
5. Write down the station, the time of arrest and the name of any Garda you dealt with.

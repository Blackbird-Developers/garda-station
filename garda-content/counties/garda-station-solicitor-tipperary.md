If you have been arrested in Tipperary, or asked to attend a Garda station for interview, you can speak to a solicitor before any questioning begins. Ring 087 122 3080 and we will tell you honestly what we can do.

## A large county with no single centre

Tipperary is the largest inland county in Ireland and it has no city and no single dominant town. That shapes how everything works here, including how far anyone has to travel to reach you.

Clonmel is the biggest town and its station carries a substantial share of custody work in the south of the county. Thurles serves the middle, and Nenagh the north. Other principal stations include Tipperary Town, Cashel, Carrick-on-Suir, Cahir and Roscrea. Templemore, which is also home to the Garda College, has its own station.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Give your name, address and date of birth and no more. Say that you want a solicitor before any interview and ask the member in charge to phone us. Ring 087 122 3080.

## Where Tipperary cases are heard

District Court sittings are held around the county, including at Clonmel, Thurles, Nenagh, Tipperary Town, Cashel, Carrick-on-Suir and Roscrea. Circuit Criminal Court business is heard at Clonmel and at Nenagh.

Because sittings are spread across so many venues, the court named on your charge sheet may not be the town you would expect. Photograph the sheet, keep it, and check the venue carefully.

## The motorway makes a real difference

Ferrys Solicitors LLP has five offices and every one of them is in Dublin. There is no Tipperary office and we are not going to imply otherwise.

That said, the geography here is more forgiving than in most of Munster. The M7 clips the north of the county, so Roscrea and Nenagh are around an hour and a half from Dublin. The M8 runs south through Cashel and Cahir, putting Cashel at roughly two hours. Clonmel and Carrick-on-Suir sit off the motorway and take longer, closer to two and a half hours.

Six criminal defence solicitors work a rota, so someone can normally take your call and give telephone advice quickly at any hour. For attendance in person we will give you a realistic time on the phone. If we cannot make it before you are interviewed, we will tell you and help you find a solicitor who can.

## Steps to take now

1. Ask the member in charge to record that you have requested a solicitor.
2. Ask for telephone advice straight away.
3. Do not discuss the allegation with anyone in the station.
4. Do not sign a statement, memo of interview or caution form until you have had advice.
5. Note the station, the time of arrest and the name of any Garda you spoke to.

## Common questions

### Which station am I likely to be brought to?

Usually the one covering the district where the alleged offence happened, which may be some distance from your home in a county this size. Ask the member in charge to confirm the station name and give it to us.

### Do you have an office in Tipperary?

No. All five offices are in Dublin. We act for clients nationwide and travel for serious matters.

### Can I get advice by phone before anyone travels?

Yes, and you should. A solicitor can speak to you in private by telephone through the member in charge, usually within a short time of you asking.

### Does the right to silence protect me completely?

You have a right to silence, but in certain limited circumstances a court may be permitted to draw inferences from a failure to answer questions. That is exactly why the decision needs legal advice first.

If you have been arrested in Longford, or asked to attend a Garda station for interview, you can speak to a solicitor before answering any questions. Ring 087 122 3080 and we will give you a straight answer about what we can do and when.

## Stations in a small county

Longford is one of the smaller counties in the country and the station network reflects that. Longford town station is the principal one and handles most of the custody work in the county.

Outside the town, arrests may be processed at Granard in the north east, Edgeworthstown on the road towards Mullingar, Ballymahon in the south, and Lanesborough on the Shannon at the Roscommon border.

Because the county is small and the population is concentrated, most people arrested in Longford end up in the town station. That is worth knowing when a family member is trying to work out where you are.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Give your name, address and date of birth, then say clearly that you want a solicitor before any interview and ask the member in charge to phone us. Say nothing else. Ring 087 122 3080.

## Where Longford cases are heard

District Court business for the county is heard at Longford Courthouse in the town centre. Cases returned for trial go to the Circuit Criminal Court, which also sits in Longford.

Having one main venue simplifies things, but it also means the list can be long. If you are given a date, allow time and arrive early.

## The distance question

Ferrys Solicitors LLP has five offices and all five are in Dublin. There is no Longford office and we are not going to pretend there is.

Longford is one of the furthest Leinster counties from us. The N4 and M4 run most of the way, and the drive is in the region of an hour and a half to an hour and three quarters in clear conditions. In the middle of the night, on a first arrest, that is a long time to sit and wait.

So here is the honest position. We field six criminal defence solicitors on a rota, so you will get a solicitor on the phone, and phone advice can start immediately. Whether we travel depends on the seriousness of the matter and who is on. For a serious charge we will make the journey. For something that needs a solicitor in the room within the hour, we will tell you that we cannot do it and help you find someone who can.

## What to do while you decide

1. Ask the member in charge to record that you have requested a solicitor.
2. Ask for telephone advice now, before any interview starts.
3. Do not discuss the allegation with anyone in the station.
4. Do not sign a statement or memo of interview until you have had advice.
5. Note the time of arrest and keep any paperwork you are given.

## Common questions

### Will you actually travel to Longford?

For serious matters, yes. For everything else we will tell you frankly whether travelling makes sense for you, and what the alternative is.

### Can you advise me by phone in the meantime?

Yes, and you should ask for that immediately. Telephone advice in the station is often the difference that matters most.

### Do you have an office in Longford?

No. Our five offices are all in Dublin. We act for clients across the country, and honesty about where we are beats a claim we cannot stand over.

### How long can I be held?

That depends on the legislation you are being held under, and detention can sometimes be extended. Ask us on the phone and we will explain what is likely to apply to you.

If you have a criminal court date in Dublin, there is a good chance it is at the Criminal Courts of Justice, the large round building on Parkgate Street in Dublin 7. Almost all criminal business in the city is heard there, from a first appearance the morning after an arrest to a trial that runs for weeks. Most people arrive not knowing where to stand or who to speak to, so this page is about the practical side of the day.

## What sits at Parkgate Street

The complex brings the criminal courts under one roof, so the same address covers very different kinds of hearing.

- The [Dublin Metropolitan District Court](/dublin-district-court): first appearances, bail, remands and summary trials.
- The [Circuit Criminal Court](/circuit-criminal-court): cases sent forward on indictment, tried before a judge and jury.
- The [Central Criminal Court](/central-criminal-court): the most serious offences.
- The [Special Criminal Court](/special-criminal-court), which sits without a jury.

Cases involving people under 18 are heard separately, at the [Children's Court in Smithfield](/childrens-court).

## Getting there and getting in

Parkgate Street runs along the north side of the Liffey near the Phoenix Park gate. Buses on the north quays stop close by, and Heuston Station and the Luas red line are a short walk away. Parking is limited.

Entry is through a screening area inside the main doors. Bags go through a scanner and you walk through a metal detector, much as at an airport. Anything sharp will not be allowed through and there is nowhere to leave belongings, so bring as little as you can. Queues build before the morning lists.

## Finding your courtroom

Court lists are displayed on screens in the main concourse, set out by court number. Find your court, take the lift or stairs to that floor, and ask court staff if you are unsure. Your solicitor will normally meet you in the concourse rather than upstairs, because that is where you can actually talk.

## What to bring

1. Your charge sheet or summons, or a photograph of it.
2. Photographic identification.
3. Anything needed for a legal aid application: PPS number, proof of income or social welfare payments.
4. Documents we have asked you for, such as a letter from an employer, a doctor or a course.
5. A note of dates you genuinely cannot attend, in case a further date is fixed.

## What the day is actually like

Lists are long and cases are not called in a predictable order. It is normal to wait several hours for a hearing that lasts two minutes. Tell work you will be out for the day rather than promising to be back by eleven.

When your case is called, come forward and let your solicitor speak. If the judge asks you something directly, answer clearly and briefly. Phones must be off, and photography and recording are not permitted anywhere in the building.

## Common questions

### What time should I get there, and what should I wear?

Arrive at least half an hour before the time on your summons. Ordinary tidy clothes are fine, but a court is a public room and it is worth looking as though you have taken the day seriously.

### Will my case be finished on the first date?

Often not. Many first appearances end with the case being put back while directions, disclosure or reports are awaited. Your solicitor will explain what the adjournment is for.

### Am I definitely in the right building?

Check the address on your summons. Some Dublin District Court business is heard outside the city centre, and matters involving under-18s go to Smithfield.

### Can somebody come in with me?

Yes. Most criminal hearings are held in public and family members can sit in the body of the court. Different rules apply in the Children's Court.

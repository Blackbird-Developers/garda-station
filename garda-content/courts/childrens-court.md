If your child has been arrested, or given a date for the Children's Court, one thing matters more than anything else here: a child should never be interviewed by Gardai without a solicitor and without a parent, guardian or other appropriate adult present. Everything else can be sorted out later. That cannot.

The Children's Court in Dublin sits at Smithfield, Dublin 7, separately from the adult criminal courts on Parkgate Street. It deals with people who were under 18 at the time of the alleged offence. Outside Dublin, the local District Court sits as the Children Court.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Tell the member in charge that your child is not to be interviewed until a solicitor has advised and an appropriate adult is present, and that you are nominating your own. Then ring 087 122 3080.

## At the Garda station: what to insist on

Children are more suggestible than adults, more likely to agree with something put to them firmly, and far more likely to say whatever will make the situation stop. That is why the safeguards exist, and why they matter even when your child has nothing to hide.

1. **Ask for a solicitor by name, out loud.** Your child is entitled to legal advice before questioning, and to a solicitor of their own choosing.
2. **Insist on an appropriate adult.** A parent or guardian should be notified and present during questioning. Where a parent cannot attend, another suitable adult should be there.
3. **Do not let a quick chat happen first.** There is no informal conversation in a Garda station.
4. **Say nothing about the allegation yourself.** You are there so your child is not alone.
5. **Sign nothing** until a solicitor has read it, including notes of interview.
6. **Mention anything relevant**: medication, a diagnosis, a learning difficulty, English not being a first language.

A voluntary attendance is still an interview, so all of this applies there too.

## How this court is different

It is a criminal court and the charges are real, but it runs on a different footing.

- **It is not open to the public.** Only people directly concerned with the case are ordinarily present.
- **Your child cannot be publicly identified.** Reporting restrictions protect anonymity, and nothing that would identify a child before the court may be published.
- **Age and welfare are central.** The court can seek reports on your child's circumstances, schooling, health and family situation.
- **Detention is a last resort,** though very serious charges still go to the adult trial courts.
- **There is a diversion route.** Many matters involving children are dealt with through the Garda youth diversion process rather than prosecution, often involving a juvenile liaison officer.

## The parent's role, and the day itself

You are expected to attend with your child, and the court can require it. Judges speak to parents directly, about supervision at home, school attendance, and who your child is spending time with. That can feel like being on trial yourself, but what the court wants is a realistic picture of the support around the child. Evidence of it helps: a letter from a school or a coach, proof of enrolment on a course.

Smithfield is on the Luas red line. Bring the summons or charge sheet, photographic identification, your child's details for legal aid, and any documents we have asked for. Prepare your child for being asked a question by the judge. Answering clearly and politely is all that is needed.

## Common questions

### The Gardai said my son can be interviewed if I am there. Do we still need a solicitor?

Yes. A parent is not a substitute for legal advice. You cannot assess whether a question should be answered, and you are frightened too.

### Will my child end up with a criminal record?

It depends on how the matter is dealt with, and it is one of the first things to raise with a solicitor. Where a case is diverted, the position differs.

### My child is 17 and turns 18 before the court date. What then?

That needs advice quickly, because age at the alleged offence and age at later stages can both matter. Ring us with the dates.

If someone has died and Gardai have arrested you, or told you they want to speak to you, ring a solicitor before you say anything at all. The investigation moves quickly in the first hours, and what you say now will be read back to you for years.

Murder concerns an unlawful killing carried out with the necessary intent. Manslaughter covers an unlawful killing where that intent is absent, and it arises in situations people never expect: a single punch outside a pub, a scuffle that got away from everyone, a fire, a fatal collision. Both are dealt with in the higher courts, and the process is long.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Say clearly that you want a solicitor and that you are not answering questions until you have spoken to one. Do not explain, apologise or set out your version to anyone at the scene. Ring 087 122 3080.

## What the Garda station stage looks like

A homicide investigation is resourced differently to anything else. Expect a scene preserved for days, a technical examination, house to house enquiries, CCTV recovered across a wide area, and phone and social media material sought early.

1. **You are arrested and detained** under whichever power applies. The period Gardai may hold you depends on the legislation used and can in some circumstances be extended, so plan for a long stay rather than a short one.
2. **Forensic samples and clothing.** Expect requests for samples and for the clothing you were wearing. There are rules about what may be taken, when consent is needed and what follows a refusal. Take advice on each request.
3. **Interviews in sessions.** There will be several, they are recorded, and they are often built around material you have not seen.
4. **Inference warnings.** In certain limited circumstances a court may be permitted to draw an inference from a failure to answer. That is why the decision to answer is a legal one, taken with a solicitor beside you.
5. **Charge or release.** You may be charged from the station, or released while the file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions.

## What happens afterwards

A murder charge comes before the District Court first, but only to be dealt with administratively. The District Court does not grant bail in a murder case; a bail application is made in the High Court. Murder is tried in the Central Criminal Court, which sits at the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street. Manslaughter is dealt with on indictment and, depending on the case, may be tried in the Circuit Criminal Court or the Central Criminal Court.

Between the first appearance and the trial there is disclosure, a book of evidence, a return for trial and usually a wait of many months. Preparation in that period, particularly around forensic and technical evidence, is a large part of the work.

## What people worry about

Almost everyone in this position fears the same three things: custody, the effect on their family, and being written about. Cases involving a death attract reporting, and your name may appear before anyone has heard your side. Your family may be approached at home. If you were the driver, licence and insurance consequences run alongside everything else. If your status here depends on a permission or a visa, that needs separate attention from the outset.

There is also the human weight of it. You may have known the person who died. Grief and shock make people talk, and talking is the thing that cannot be undone.

## Common questions

### The Gardai say they only want a statement to help the investigation. Is that different?

Not in practice. A witness account can become a suspect account very quickly. Ring us before you attend, even voluntarily.

### They have taken my clothes and swabs. Have I lost my defence?

No. Samples are a routine part of these investigations. What matters is that each request is dealt with properly and that you understand what is being asked.

### How long before I know if I am being charged?

Sometimes within the detention period, often much later, because the file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions.

### Can my family visit me while I am in the station?

Someone can be notified of your detention, but access is limited. Your solicitor is the reliable line of contact and can update your family on your instructions.

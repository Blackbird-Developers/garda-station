If you have been arrested, or asked to come to a Garda station, in connection with a sexual offence allegation, take legal advice before you say a word about it. This is the category of case where an unadvised conversation does the most lasting damage, and where the consequences begin long before any court date.

These allegations cover a wide range of conduct and circumstances. Many concern people who know each other. Many involve alcohol and disputed recollection of an evening. A significant number are historical, raised years or decades afterwards. In all of them, the account you give in the first interview will be examined closely for the rest of the case.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Say that you want a solicitor and will not answer questions until you have spoken to one. Say nothing about the allegation to anyone. Ring 087 122 3080.

## Do not discuss the allegation with anyone

This matters more here than in almost any other case.

- **Do not contact the complainant.** Not to apologise, not to ask what is going on, not through a third party. Contact of any kind can create a separate and serious difficulty, and it will almost certainly be a bail condition.
- **Do not message mutual friends or family** about what is alleged. Those messages can be recovered and read out.
- **Do not post anything online,** and do not delete anything either. Deletion creates its own problems.
- **Do not rehearse it with anyone but your solicitor.** Conversations with a solicitor are confidential. Conversations with everyone else are not.

## What the Garda station stage looks like

Contact often begins with a call inviting you to attend voluntarily. A voluntary attendance is still an interview.

1. **Take advice before you attend or, if arrested, before questioning.** Ask for a solicitor out loud.
2. **Expect disclosure to be limited.** You may be given only a general outline of what is alleged before questioning starts. In historical cases you may be asked about a period of years rather than a date.
3. **Samples, devices and clothing.** Depending on the allegation, requests may be made for samples, for your phone or for items from your home. Take advice on each.
4. **Long interviews, often over several sessions,** covering the relationship, the circumstances and any communications between you.
5. **Inference warnings.** In certain limited circumstances a court may be permitted to draw an inference from a failure to answer. Whether to answer is a legal decision.
6. **Charge or release** while a file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions. That wait can be long.

## What happens afterwards

Rape and aggravated sexual assault are tried in the Central Criminal Court at Parkgate Street. Other sexual offences are commonly dealt with in the Circuit Criminal Court, and some in the District Court, depending on the charge and on prosecution decisions.

Bail conditions tend to be strict: no contact directly or indirectly, an address to reside at, sometimes conditions about where you may go or who you may live with. Where children are involved, family law or child protection processes may run alongside the criminal case.

## What people worry about

The reputational consequences are felt first. Employers may suspend pending the outcome. If you hold Garda vetting, or are registered with a professional body in teaching, healthcare, social care or sport, notification obligations may arise and should be checked before you disclose anything. Travel and visa applications can be affected, and if you are living here on a permission, immigration advice should run in parallel.

There are protections around identification in certain cases before the courts, and how they apply to yours is worth asking about early. Family life is affected immediately, particularly where a bail condition changes where you live.

## Common questions

### The allegation is from more than twenty years ago. Does that change anything?

Historical allegations are investigated and prosecuted. The passage of time raises particular issues that need to be identified early, so tell your solicitor the dates and what you recall.

### The Gardai asked me to come in for a chat and said I am not under arrest.

Attend only after you have spoken to a solicitor. A voluntary interview is recorded and used like any other.

### Should I tell my employer?

Take advice before you tell anyone. Your obligations depend on your contract, your sector and whether you are vetted or registered.

### My phone has been taken. What happens to it?

It will be examined, and that can take time. Ask your solicitor what has been seized.

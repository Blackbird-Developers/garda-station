Assault allegations usually come out of a few seconds that everyone involved remembers differently. A push at a bar, a row that turned physical, a confrontation over a parking space, an incident at home. If you have been arrested, or asked to come in and give your side, speak to a solicitor first.

Assault covers a ladder of allegations, from an assault causing no significant injury, up through assault causing harm, to assault causing serious harm. There are also specific charges for assaults on Gardai and other emergency workers. Which rung a case sits on frequently changes after arrest, when medical evidence arrives, so an interview given on the assumption that this is minor can be a serious mistake.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Do not describe what happened, even to say that you were defending yourself. Ask for a solicitor and wait. Ring 087 122 3080.

## What the Garda station stage looks like

1. **Arrest, often on the night,** sometimes hours later once a complaint has been made and a statement taken.
2. **Injuries are recorded.** Photographs may be taken of the injured party and of you. If you were injured, say so and ask to be examined by a doctor. People regularly forget this.
3. **Footage.** In town centres and licensed premises there is usually CCTV, and often phone video as well.
4. **Interview.** Expect questions about who started it, what was said beforehand, how many blows there were and whether you had been drinking.
5. **Self defence is not a magic word.** Whether it arises depends on the detail, and a half explained account given without advice tends to become an admission with an explanation attached.
6. **Charge or release.** Many people are released while medical reports are obtained and a file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions, then charged or summonsed later.

Where both people were injured and both have complained, the position needs careful handling.

## What happens afterwards

Lower level assaults are dealt with in the District Court. Assault causing harm may stay in the District Court or be sent forward to the Circuit Criminal Court, depending on the direction of the Director of Public Prosecutions. Allegations of causing serious harm are dealt with on indictment before a judge and jury. In Dublin, these courts sit at the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street.

If the incident happened at home or between family members, expect bail conditions about contact and, in some cases, about where you may live. Separate civil applications for protection or safety orders, and any child protection process, run on their own track. Your solicitor needs to know about them. A personal injuries claim may also follow later.

## What people worry about

Assault convictions are treated by employers as an indicator of risk. Garda vetting affects work with children and vulnerable adults. Nurses, doctors, teachers and social care staff have registration bodies with their own requirements. Security staff and taxi drivers hold licences that are reviewed. Sports governing bodies often run their own disciplinary process after a public incident.

Visa and entry questions for the United States, Canada and Australia ask about convictions involving violence. If your permission to remain in Ireland is due for renewal, take advice on how a prosecution interacts with it. And if you are a parent, bail conditions about contact and residence can affect arrangements for your children before anything has been decided about the allegation.

## Common questions

### He hit me first. Should I not just explain that?

Explain it to your solicitor. How and when that is put forward matters, and the first interview is rarely the place to work it out alone.

### The other person says they are dropping the charges.

Complaints are not withdrawn in the way people expect. The decision to prosecute rests with the State.

### I was charged with a minor assault but now I hear it is more serious.

That happens when medical evidence arrives. Get advice immediately, because the court dealing with the case may change.

### Can I contact the other person to sort it out?

No. Contact can lead to a further allegation and will usually breach bail conditions. Leave it to your solicitor.

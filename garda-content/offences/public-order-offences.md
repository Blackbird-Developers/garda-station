For most people who ever see the inside of a Garda station, this is the reason. A night out, drink taken, a row at a taxi rank, a raised voice at a Garda, and suddenly you are in the back of a van. It is a common arrest, and it never feels ordinary.

Public order offences cover behaviour in a public place: being intoxicated to the point of being a danger to yourself or others, disorderly conduct, threatening, abusive or insulting behaviour, obstruction, refusing to leave a premises, and failing to comply with a Garda direction. Many arrests involve more than one of these, and things said during the arrest often become part of the charge.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Say nothing further, do not argue on the street and do not pull away. Ask for a solicitor. If you are ringing on someone else's behalf, ring 087 122 3080.

## What happens in the station

1. **You are brought before the member in charge,** your details are taken and your property recorded. Tell them about any medical condition, medication or injury, and about anyone who should know where you are.
2. **You may be held until you are sober.** In a straightforward public order matter there may be no formal interview, or a short one.
3. **Everything you say is noted,** including in the van and the cell. Abuse directed at Gardai in the station can lead to a further charge.
4. **You are charged and released, or released pending a summons.** Where you are charged in the station, you are usually released on station bail with a District Court date.
5. **Read the charge sheet before you leave** and photograph it. People lose it, then miss a court date, which is a far bigger problem than the charge.
6. **Ring a solicitor the next morning,** even if you feel foolish. Especially if you feel foolish.

If matters escalated during the arrest, this can become an allegation of resisting arrest, obstruction, criminal damage or assault on a Garda. Those need advice quickly.

## What happens afterwards

Public order charges are dealt with in the District Court, usually a few weeks later. In Dublin that is the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street. The hearing is short, the list is long, and the case often turns on a Garda's account of what was said, sometimes with CCTV or body worn footage.

Attend on the date on your charge sheet, in ordinary decent clothes, with identification and, if you may apply for legal aid, details of your means. If you cannot attend, tell your solicitor in advance. A warrant for failing to appear is much harder to fix.

## What people worry about

The worry is rarely the fine. It is what a conviction does afterwards.

- **Work and vetting.** Anyone applying for Garda vetting, or working in healthcare, education, security or childcare, will be asked about convictions. Security staff and taxi drivers hold licences with their own checks.
- **Travel.** Visa and entry questions for the United States, Canada and Australia ask about convictions and sometimes about arrests. Answering wrongly creates a separate problem.
- **Immigration permission.** If you are here on a student or work permission, a criminal matter can surface at renewal.
- **Study and professional entry.** Some courses, professional bodies and graduate employers ask directly.
- **Being named.** District Court lists are public and reported.

## Common questions

### I was let go in the morning with no charge sheet. Is that the end of it?

Not always. A summons can issue afterwards and arrive by post weeks later.

### I do not remember what I said. Does that matter?

The Gardai will have notes and there may be footage. Your solicitor can find out what is alleged before you say anything further.

### Can I just pay a fine and avoid court?

There are fixed charge procedures for some matters, but a charge sheet with a court date requires attendance. Ring us and we will tell you which you have.

### I am not from Ireland and I have a flight home next week.

Tell your solicitor immediately. Travel and court dates can sometimes be managed, but only if raised beforehand.

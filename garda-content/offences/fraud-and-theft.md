Fraud and theft allegations tend to arrive without a dramatic arrest. More often it is a letter, a suspension from work, a call from a Garda asking you to come in, or a security officer holding you at the door of a shop. The instinct in each case is to explain yourself. Take advice first.

This area covers a wide range of everyday allegations: taking goods from a shop, taking money or stock from an employer, using someone else's card or account details, obtaining money or services by deception, and handling stolen property. What links them is that the case usually turns on dishonest intent, and intent is what an unadvised interview is used to establish.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Do not offer an explanation, do not hand over documents and do not offer to repay anything. Ask for a solicitor and answer no questions until you have spoken to one. Ring 087 122 3080.

## Where these cases come from

- **Retail.** Detention by store security, CCTV, till and stock records, then Gardai called in.
- **The workplace.** An internal audit or disciplinary process, sometimes months of it, followed by a complaint to the Gardai. Anything you said internally can end up in the Garda file.
- **Banking and cards.** Chargebacks, transaction records and account activity, with the account holder or the recipient of a transfer asked to explain it.
- **Money moving through an account.** People who let someone else use their account, or who received and passed on funds, are often questioned about handling or laundering.

## What the Garda station stage looks like

1. **Often a voluntary attendance first.** You may be invited in by appointment rather than arrested. It is still a recorded interview.
2. **A documents case.** Expect statements, receipts, rosters, CCTV stills and transaction lists to be put to you page by page.
3. **Devices and accounts.** Phones and laptops may be seized, and banking records sought.
4. **Questions designed to establish intent,** not just what happened. Answers such as "I was going to put it back" are often more damaging than silence.
5. **Do not repay anything at this stage.** Offering restitution before you have advice can be treated as an admission. If repayment is appropriate, timing and wording matter.
6. **Release, then a decision** on charge, often after a file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions.

## What happens afterwards

Most theft and lower value fraud matters are dealt with in the District Court, in Dublin at the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street. Larger, more complex or multi count fraud allegations may be sent forward to the Circuit Criminal Court, before a judge and jury. Documentary cases move slowly, because disclosure runs to volumes rather than pages.

A civil claim by an employer, or a live disciplinary process, runs alongside the criminal case. Tell your solicitor about every parallel one.

## What people worry about

For most clients the criminal case is not the first fear. The job is. Dishonesty allegations affect references, and carry particular weight in retail, banking, insurance, accountancy, healthcare and any role handling cash or client money. Regulated staff in financial services face fitness and probity requirements, and anyone working with children or vulnerable adults will be thinking about Garda vetting.

Visa applications and entry to the United States, Canada and Australia can also be affected by convictions involving dishonesty. If you are living here on a permission, take advice early. Court lists are public, and local reporting worries anyone whose name is known in a small community.

## Common questions

### Store security have my details and said Gardai will be in touch. What do I do?

Ring a solicitor now rather than waiting. Early advice shapes what happens at the interview, the stage that matters most.

### If I pay the money back, will the case go away?

Nobody can promise that, and repaying money before advice can be read as an admission of dishonesty. Discuss it with your solicitor first.

### My employer wants me at a disciplinary meeting while the investigation is ongoing.

Get advice before you attend. What you say in an internal process is not confidential and can be passed to the Gardai.

### Someone used my account to move money and I did not know. Am I in trouble?

You may still be questioned about handling or laundering. Say nothing until you have spoken to a solicitor.

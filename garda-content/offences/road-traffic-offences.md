Road traffic cases are the ones people least expect to need a solicitor for, and the ones where the consequences most often land on the part of life that pays the bills. A checkpoint on the way home, a collision on a wet road, a phone in your hand at the wrong moment, and the issue is no longer a fine. It is whether you can drive.

The range is wide: driving over the limit or under the influence of drugs, refusing or failing to provide a specimen, dangerous or careless driving, no insurance or licence, failing to remain at the scene, and the serious charges that follow a collision causing death or injury.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Be co-operative and polite, do not argue at the roadside, and do not explain how much you had or what you took. Ask for a solicitor. Ring 087 122 3080.

## What happens at the station

1. **Arrest at a checkpoint or after a collision,** then transfer to the station and appearance before the member in charge.
2. **You are told what is required of you** and asked for a specimen. Failing or refusing is treated as seriously as the allegation itself.
3. **Breath.** A breath specimen is taken on an evidential machine after a period of observation, and you sign a printed statement.
4. **Blood or urine.** Where a doctor or nurse attends, the requirement may be for blood or, in some circumstances, urine, and there is sometimes a choice. One part of the sample is offered to you. Keep it safe.
5. **Drug driving.** Roadside testing and an impairment assessment come first, then a specimen at the station. Analysis takes time, so people are often released and charged later.
6. **Ring for advice straight away.** You are entitled to legal advice, though the specimen procedure runs to its own timing and a solicitor may not always reach you before a specimen is required.

Keep everything you are given, and write down times, names and the order of events.

## What happens afterwards

Most road traffic prosecutions are heard in the District Court for the area where the alleged offence occurred, so a checkpoint on the way home can mean a court date far from home. In Dublin, District Court criminal business sits at the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street, and a collision causing death or serious injury goes before a judge and jury in the Circuit Criminal Court.

You may be charged in the station, or receive a summons months later where analysis was required. Fixed charge notices and penalty points run separately, with their own deadlines.

## What people worry about

For many clients the case is really about the licence. Losing it affects sales representatives, tradespeople, delivery drivers, carers, healthcare staff on call and anyone commuting from a place with no bus, and a vocational licence brings separate consequences to check.

Insurance is the next worry: the cost of cover, whether cover can be had at all, and what you must declare to your insurer and to any employer who provides a vehicle. A conviction may also have to be declared when you travel.

## Common questions

### I was released with no charge after a drug driving arrest. Is it finished?

Probably not. Specimens go for analysis and a summons can follow months later. Keep your address current and keep our number.

### Do I have to answer questions about how much I had to drink?

You must comply with the specimen procedure. That is not the same as giving an account of your evening, a separate decision to take with a solicitor.

### I need my licence for work. Can that be taken into account?

Your circumstances can be put before the court, but nobody can say in advance what a court will do. Tell your solicitor early so anything relevant is prepared properly.

### The summons is for a court in another county. Do I have to go?

Yes, cases are heard where the alleged offence occurred. We act for clients nationwide and travel for court, so ring us with the summons in front of you.

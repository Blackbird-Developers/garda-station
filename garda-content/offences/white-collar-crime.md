White collar cases rarely start with a knock at the door at six in the morning. Far more often they start quietly: a request for documents, an unexpected audit, a colleague being questioned, a letter from a regulator, or a call inviting you to a Garda station by appointment. By the time most people ring a solicitor, the investigation has been running a while.

This area covers allegations connected to business and money: fraud within a company, false accounting, misuse of company funds, tax and Revenue matters, market conduct, procurement, bribery and money laundering. Investigations may be led by Gardai, by Revenue, by a financial or corporate regulator, or by more than one at once.

## An investigation, not an arrest

The distinctive feature of these matters is that the critical decisions are made long before anyone is charged, and usually before anyone is arrested.

- **The company's lawyers are not your lawyers.** If you are asked to attend an interview run by the company or a firm it has engaged, that lawyer acts for the company. Your interests may diverge quickly. Get your own advice first.
- **Regulatory interviews are not Garda interviews.** In some regulatory processes there are obligations to co-operate and to answer. How answers given in one process may be used in another is a technical question, and it must be worked out before you speak.
- **Document requests are decisions.** What is produced, in what form, and whether anything is privileged, all matter. Sending everything to make the problem go away rarely works.
- **Parallel processes.** A criminal investigation, a regulatory process, a disciplinary procedure, a civil claim and an insurance notification can all be live at once. Something said to satisfy one can damage another.

## What the Garda station stage looks like

1. **Arrest is often arranged.** A date and time are frequently agreed in advance, and your solicitor attends with you.
2. **Searches of premises and devices.** Company servers, email accounts, laptops and phones may be taken. Ask at once about privileged material.
3. **Documents put to you page by page.** Interviews are long, technical and built on material gathered over months.
4. **Questions about knowledge and authority:** what you knew, what you approved, what you signed, who reported to you. Answers given from memory about events years ago carry real risk.
5. **Release, then a long wait** while the file is completed and the Director of Public Prosecutions considers it.

## What happens afterwards

Charges of this kind are commonly dealt with on indictment before a judge and jury in the Circuit Criminal Court, with lower value matters remaining in the District Court. In Dublin, both sit at the Criminal Courts of Justice on Parkgate Street. Timelines are long and disclosure is voluminous.

## What people worry about

The professional consequences usually frighten clients more than the criminal ones. Fitness and probity requirements apply to regulated roles in financial services. Accountants, solicitors, auditors, pharmacists and other registered professionals face obligations to their regulators, and reporting duties can be triggered by an investigation rather than any finding. Directors worry about their board positions, and about disqualification and restriction processes that run separately from the criminal case.

There are practical worries too: whether to tell your employer, what to say to your bank, whether you can travel for work, and what happens if your name appears in a court list. If your permission to remain here is tied to employment, that needs attention too.

## Common questions

### I have received a request for documents. Do I have to hand everything over?

The scope, the format of production and whether any material is privileged all need to be considered. Take advice before you send anything.

### Should I resign?

That decision carries criminal, employment and regulatory consequences at once. Do not take it in a hurry.

### The company says the interview is confidential and internal. Is it?

Do not assume so. Material generated in an internal investigation can end up with investigators. You are entitled to your own advice.

### Nothing has happened for a year. Is the investigation over?

Not necessarily. These files take a long time. We can contact the investigating body to establish where matters stand.

Most arrests do not happen between nine and five. They happen on Friday nights, at closing time, on Sunday mornings, on a bank holiday Monday when nothing is open and every phone rings out. That is exactly when a person is least likely to get advice, and most likely to be interviewed without it. We answer 087 122 3080 outside office hours, at weekends and on public holidays.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> It does not matter what time it is. Say you want legal advice before any interview and that you are naming your own solicitor, then ring 087 122 3080.

## A rota of six, not one mobile phone

Round the clock cover means very little if it rests on one person being awake, free and within reach. One phone can only be in one place. If that person is already in an interview room in one station, at a hearing, asleep after a night shift, or simply unreachable, the availability is theoretical.

Ferrys Solicitors LLP fields six criminal defence solicitors: Tony Collier, Fiona D'Arcy, Holly Laher, Katie Kavanagh, Michael McNieve and Cordina Serbanescu, working across five Dublin offices. Out of hours cover is organised between them as a rota.

That has three practical consequences on a Saturday night.

- **Two calls at once are not a problem.** One solicitor can be at a station in Dublin 7 while another takes a call from Dublin 10.
- **Somebody is on duty by arrangement,** not by chance, so the person who answers is expecting the call.
- **A long detention can be covered properly.** If a matter runs from Saturday night into a Sunday court sitting, the work can be handed on within a team that already knows the file.

The firm has been practising since 1989, and criminal defence is a settled part of what it does rather than an out of hours sideline.

## Courts sit at weekends too

People are often surprised that a person charged on a Saturday does not simply wait until Monday. Where somebody is charged and is not released on station bail, they must be brought before a court, and arrangements exist for sittings outside normal court days, including at weekends and over bank holiday periods.

A weekend sitting is short and it moves quickly. The charge is put, bail is dealt with, and a date is fixed. Bail is often the whole substance of the hearing, and it is where preparation shows: a proposed address, an independent surety who can attend, employment or college details, and a realistic set of conditions.

If a family member is in custody and a weekend sitting is likely, ring us before it rather than after. See [a family member being arrested](/family-member-arrested).

## What to do at three in the morning

1. **Ring 087 122 3080.** Say where the station is and whether an interview has started.
2. **If you are the person in custody,** ask the member in charge to let you contact your solicitor and say you are not answering questions until you have.
3. **If you are ringing for somebody else,** have the station name, the person's full name and date of birth, and anything relevant about their health, age or language.
4. **If the line is engaged,** it is because a colleague is dealing with another station. Leave a voicemail with a number, or send a WhatsApp message, and keep your phone in your hand.
5. **Do not wait until morning to make the first call.** The interview will not wait until morning.

## If you cannot get through immediately

Nights get busy and there are moments when every solicitor on the rota is already engaged. If that happens:

- **Leave a message with a callback number.** We work through them in order of urgency, and a person facing an imminent interview goes to the top.
- **Send a WhatsApp message** to the same number if a call will not connect. Include the station and the person's name.
- **Say nothing about the allegation in the meantime.** Waiting for advice is not an admission of anything.
- **Ask the member in charge to record** that you have requested a solicitor and are waiting for one.
- **Do not accept an interview now on the basis that a solicitor can be sorted out afterwards.** Afterwards is too late for the recording that already exists.

## Being honest about distance

We are a Dublin firm with five Dublin offices, and we act for clients around the country. If you ring us at three in the morning from a station a long way from Dublin, we will tell you honestly whether we can attend in time to be of use.

If we cannot, we will still give you advice on the phone straight away, because that is the part that cannot wait, and we will help you find a solicitor who can get to you. We would rather do that than let somebody sit in a cell waiting for a car that will arrive after the interview is over.

## Common questions

### Will somebody actually answer at four in the morning?

Yes. 087 122 3080 is covered outside office hours by whichever of our criminal defence solicitors is on the rota. If the line is engaged, leave a message or send a WhatsApp and keep your phone with you.

### Can a solicitor come to the station at night, or is it only phone advice?

Both are possible. Advice by telephone happens immediately, because that is what stops an unadvised interview. Attendance in person depends on the station, the time and the nature of the matter, and we will tell you plainly what to expect rather than promising a car that is not coming.

### My son was arrested on Saturday. Will he be held until Monday?

Not necessarily. He may be released without charge, released on station bail with a court date, or brought before a sitting of the court, and sittings are arranged outside normal court days where needed. Ring 087 122 3080 with his name, date of birth and the station.

### Does it cost more to call out of hours?

The Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme is means tested and administered by the Legal Aid Board, and it does not have a separate night rate. Cost is not a reason to be interviewed alone at two in the morning. See [legal aid](/legal-aid).

### What about Christmas and bank holidays?

The rota covers bank holidays and the Christmas period. Those are among the busiest times of the year in Garda stations, and they are exactly when a person is most likely to be told that nothing can be done until offices reopen.

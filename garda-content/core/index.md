If you have been arrested, or a Garda has asked you to come into a station for a chat, the most useful thing you can do is speak to a solicitor before you answer any questions. What you say in a Garda station is written down, often recorded, and it can be read back to you in a courtroom many months later.

Advice at the station is free of charge for most people, and it is available at any hour, including tonight. Our number is 087 122 3080.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Tell the member in charge, calmly, that you want legal advice before any interview and that you are nominating your own solicitor. Then ring 087 122 3080.

## What to do in the first few minutes

Fear makes people talk. The urge to explain yourself, to clear this up now and get home, is completely normal, and it causes most of the damage we see afterwards.

1. **Ask for a solicitor out loud, and name one.** Say you want legal advice before any questioning. Give our name and number: Ferrys Solicitors, 087 122 3080.
2. **Do not discuss the allegation with anyone.** There is no such thing as an off the record conversation in a Garda station.
3. **Give your basic details.** Name, address and date of birth are ordinarily required. That is a different thing from answering questions about an allegation.
4. **Wait for your advice before the interview starts.** If questioning begins first, say again, clearly, that you want your solicitor.
5. **Sign nothing** until a solicitor has been through it with you, including notes of interview and statements.
6. **Tell the member in charge about anything medical**: medication, a condition, an injury, pregnancy, withdrawal, a mental health difficulty.

## The rights you have in a Garda station

These are entitlements, not favours, and asking for them does not make you look guilty.

- **A solicitor of your own choosing.** You need not take whoever is suggested to you.
- **Legal advice before questioning,** in private, by phone or in person.
- **A solicitor present during the interview** itself, in the ordinary course.
- **A right to silence,** although in certain limited circumstances a court may be permitted to draw inferences from a failure to answer particular questions. That is precisely why the decision needs advice, not a guess made alone in a cell.
- **The presumption of innocence.** The case must be proved against you.
- **Proper treatment in custody,** with a record kept of what happens and when.
- **Someone told where you are.**
- **An interpreter,** and **a doctor** where either is needed.
- **Additional protections if you are under 18.**

Each of these is explained on our page about [your rights in a Garda station](/your-rights).

## How long you can be held

Detention exists so an investigation can be carried out. The maximum period depends on the legislation you are held under, and it can sometimes be extended. Time can also be paused, for rest overnight for example, so the clock on the wall is not always the clock that counts.

Detention ordinarily ends in one of four ways: released without charge; charged and released on station bail with a court date; brought before the next sitting of the District Court; or released while a file goes to the Director of Public Prosecutions. Those routes have very different consequences, and they are set out on [what happens after arrest](/arrested).

## Common questions

### Will asking for a solicitor make me look guilty?

No. It is an ordinary entitlement, exercised every day by people who are never charged with anything, and Gardai are entirely used to it. The people who come to regret their time in custody are rarely the ones who asked for advice.

### Does advice at the station cost anything?

For most people, no. The Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme is means tested and administered by the Legal Aid Board. Ask us when you ring. There is more on our [legal aid page](/legal-aid).

### I have only been asked in for a chat. Do I need this?

An invitation to attend voluntarily is still an invitation to be interviewed, and what you say is treated the same way. People are sometimes asked in as a witness and find the questions change direction. See [attending voluntarily](/voluntary-attendance).

### It is the middle of the night. Will anyone answer?

Yes. 087 122 3080 is answered outside office hours, at weekends and on bank holidays by whichever of our criminal defence solicitors is on call. If the line is engaged, leave a voicemail or send a WhatsApp message and keep the phone with you.

### I am the parent, not the person arrested. Can I ring?

Yes, and often it is a parent or partner who makes the first call. Tell us who has been arrested, which station they are in, and anything we should know about their health, age or language.

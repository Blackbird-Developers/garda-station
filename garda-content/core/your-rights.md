Everything on this page is an entitlement, not a favour, and asking for any of it does not count against you. People in custody often stay quiet about what they need, either because they do not want to cause trouble or because they assume asking makes things worse. It does not. If you are unsure, ring 087 122 3080.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Say clearly to the member in charge that you want legal advice before any interview and that you are choosing your own solicitor. Then ring 087 122 3080.

## A solicitor of your own choosing

You choose your solicitor. You do not have to accept the first name suggested to you, and you are not obliged to use someone because they are convenient or already at the station.

If you have a name and number, give it. If you do not, you can ask for a list, but you remain free to name a firm you have heard of. If a relative has already instructed a solicitor for you, that solicitor can act once you confirm it.

## Legal advice before you are questioned

You are entitled to consult a solicitor before an interview begins, and to do so in private. That consultation can happen by telephone, which is why it can be arranged quickly at any hour, and a solicitor may also attend in person.

Use it properly. Tell your solicitor what you have been told, what has been put to you, whether anything has been seized, and whether you have already said anything. The advice is only as good as the information you give.

## A solicitor present during the interview

In the ordinary course you are entitled to have a solicitor present during the questioning itself, not just beforehand. A solicitor in the room does not answer for you. They are there to see that the questioning is conducted properly, to intervene if it is not, to ask for a break when something new is put to you, and to keep a note.

## The right to silence, and its limits

You have a right to silence. You are not required to answer questions about an allegation, and the burden of proof rests on the prosecution.

That right is not absolute. In certain limited circumstances a court may be permitted to draw inferences from a failure or refusal to answer particular questions, or to account for particular things. Whether that arises depends on the circumstances of the case, and it is not something to work out for yourself under pressure.

This is the strongest single reason to get advice before an interview. The choice is rarely a simple one between talking and saying nothing. It may involve a prepared statement, answering some matters and not others, or asking for disclosure of what is alleged before responding.

## The presumption of innocence

You are presumed innocent. The prosecution must prove its case, and you do not have to disprove anything. That has a practical consequence in the station: you are not obliged to construct an alibi, produce documents, or explain away everything put to you in order to be released. Detention ends whether or not you have satisfied anybody.

## How you must be treated in custody

Rules govern the treatment of people in Garda custody, and a member in charge is responsible for seeing that they are observed. In broad terms you are entitled to be treated with dignity, to reasonable access to food, drink and rest, and to be held in reasonable conditions.

A custody record is kept, noting when you arrived, the reason for arrest, when you were interviewed and fed, and when you asked for a solicitor. It matters later, so tell your solicitor about anything that felt wrong, however small.

## Someone being told where you are

You are entitled to have a person you nominate notified of your detention and of where you are held. That is usually a family member or partner, and it is worth asking for early. A foreign national may also be entitled to have their consulate or embassy notified.

## An interpreter

If you do not speak English well enough to follow an interview and be understood in it, you are entitled to an interpreter, and questioning should not proceed as though you understand when you do not. Do not be polite about this. Nodding along because you catch most of it is a serious risk where the exact wording of an answer may matter.

## Medical attention

If you are unwell, injured, pregnant, withdrawing from alcohol or drugs, or you take prescribed medication, tell the member in charge immediately. You are entitled to medical attention where it is needed and a doctor can be called to the station.

This includes mental health. If you have a diagnosed condition, or you are in crisis, say so and ask that it be noted.

## If you are under 18

A child in custody has additional protections. In general terms a parent or guardian should be notified and, where possible, present during questioning, and a child should not be interviewed without an appropriate adult. Age also affects what happens after the station stage, including whether a matter is dealt with through the Garda Youth Diversion Programme rather than the courts.

## If a right is not respected

1. **Ask again, clearly, and ask for it to be recorded.** Use plain words: "I want a solicitor before I answer any questions."
2. **Note the time** as best you can, and who you asked.
3. **Do not become obstructive.** Refusing your name, or resisting, creates separate difficulties.
4. **Do not fill the gap by talking.** An unmet request is no reason to start answering.
5. **Tell your solicitor everything about it,** including anything said outside the interview.

## Common questions

### Can I be interviewed before I have spoken to a solicitor?

You are entitled to legal advice before questioning and you should say that you want it. If an interview begins anyway, repeat the request and do not treat the refusal as a reason to start answering. Tell your solicitor exactly what happened and when.

### Can I be searched, and can they take my phone?

Searches, seizure of property and the taking of samples are all possible in custody, with different rules attaching to each. Some require consent, some do not, and refusing can carry consequences. Ask your solicitor before agreeing to anything. See [after arrest](/arrested).

### Does a solicitor cost me money at this stage?

For most people the Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme covers advice in the station. It is means tested and administered by the Legal Aid Board. See [legal aid](/legal-aid).

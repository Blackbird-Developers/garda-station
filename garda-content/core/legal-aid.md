Cost stops people asking for a solicitor, and it should not. For most people, legal advice in a Garda station is free at the point of use and available at any hour. If money is the reason you are hesitating, ring 087 122 3080 and ask us before you go into an interview alone.

## Free legal advice in the Garda station

The Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme provides for a solicitor to advise a person who is detained in a Garda station. It is administered by the Legal Aid Board and it is means tested, so it depends on your financial circumstances rather than on the nature of the allegation.

In practical terms it covers the advice you need when you need it: a consultation before questioning, and attendance at the station where that is appropriate. A few points worth knowing:

- **You do not need to arrange anything in advance.** The application is dealt with as part of the process and your solicitor will guide you through the form.
- **It is means tested,** so your income and circumstances are relevant. Many people qualify.
- **Do not assume you are ineligible because you are working.** Ask at the station, or ask us.
- **Advice comes first.** Nobody should be delaying it over paperwork.

If you are unsure whether you qualify, that is not a reason to be interviewed without a solicitor.

## Criminal legal aid in court

Criminal legal aid is a separate scheme, and it applies to representation in court proceedings. It is granted by the court, and the court applies two tests together.

1. **Means.** Your financial circumstances must be such that you cannot afford to pay for legal representation yourself. You will ordinarily be asked to complete a statement of means, and you should complete it honestly and fully.
2. **Gravity.** The charge must be serious enough, or the circumstances exceptional enough, to justify legal aid in the interests of justice. Where a real risk of imprisonment arises, that is a significant factor.

If legal aid is granted, a certificate is assigned and your solicitor is paid under the scheme rather than by you. If a matter proceeds to a higher court, or if counsel is needed, the certificate may be extended to cover that.

### How it is applied for

1. **Tell your solicitor before your first court date** that you will be seeking legal aid.
2. **Bring what you have** about your circumstances: proof of income or social welfare payments, rent or mortgage details, dependants.
3. **The application is made in court,** usually at the first appearance, and the judge decides.
4. **Complete the statement of means accurately.** It is a formal document.
5. **If it is refused, ask your solicitor what happens next,** including whether the position can be revisited if circumstances change or if the case becomes more serious.

## You still choose your own solicitor

This is the part people most often do not realise. Legal aid does not mean you are allocated a solicitor. You choose who represents you, and the certificate is assigned to the solicitor you have chosen, provided they are registered on the relevant panel.

That applies at the station stage and in court. If you already have a solicitor you trust, name them. If a family member has instructed somebody on your behalf, you can confirm that choice. And if you have instructed a solicitor and later decide you would prefer a different one, you are entitled to change, subject to the rules that apply to transferring a legal aid certificate. That is simply a statement of your right to choose your own representation, and nothing more should be read into it.

## If you do not qualify

Some people will not meet the means test, and a smaller number will have charges that do not meet the gravity test in court. You are not left without options.

- **Ask for a clear fee position in writing,** so that you know what work is proposed and what it costs before you commit to it.
- **The station question is separate from the court question.** Not qualifying for one does not decide the other.
- **Circumstances change.** If your income falls, or the charge becomes more serious, the position can be revisited.
- **Do not let it stop the first phone call.** Initial contact to work out where you stand is not something we bill you for.

## Common questions

### Is legal advice at the Garda station really free?

For most people, yes. The Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme is means tested and administered by the Legal Aid Board, and it covers advice and attendance at the station stage. Ask about it when you ring, and do not go into an interview unadvised while you are working it out.

### Does asking for the free scheme mean I get whoever is available?

No. You choose your own solicitor under the scheme in the same way as you would if you were paying privately. Name the firm you want.

### I was refused legal aid in court. What do I do now?

Speak to your solicitor about why. It may be a question of the means information provided, and it may be capable of being revisited, particularly if your circumstances change or if the charge becomes more serious. There may also be a private fee arrangement that is proportionate to the work involved.

### Do I have to pay anything back later?

Criminal legal aid, where it is granted, is not a loan. A person who is found to have given false or misleading information in an application, however, exposes themselves to consequences. Complete the statement of means honestly.

### I already have a solicitor but I want to change. Can I?

You are entitled to choose who represents you, at any stage. Where a legal aid certificate has already been assigned, there are rules about transferring it, and your new solicitor can explain the position. It is a decision for you and there is no obligation to explain your reasons to us.

### Does legal aid cover a barrister?

In more serious matters, or where a case goes to a higher court, a legal aid certificate may be extended to cover counsel. Whether that arises depends on the court and the charge.

You have had a phone call, or a knock at the door, or your child has not come home and somebody has told you where they are. The instinct is to do something immediately. Some of what you can do genuinely helps. Some of it, done with the best intentions, causes real damage. This page is about the difference. If you want to talk it through now, ring 087 122 3080.

> **If the Gardai are waiting**
> Ring the station, ask for the member in charge, and say that a solicitor is being instructed for the person in custody. Then ring 087 122 3080.

## What you can find out

Ring the Garda station and ask to speak to the member in charge, the Garda responsible for people in custody there. Ask, calmly:

- whether the person is in that station
- that they be told you have rung
- that a solicitor is being instructed, giving our name and number
- whether they have been given legal advice yet
- whether an interview has taken place or is about to

You may not be told very much. Details of an investigation will not be discussed with you, and that is not obstruction, it is how it works. What matters is that the message about a solicitor gets through, because the person in custody has the right to a solicitor of their own choosing and your call makes that choice a real one. If you do not know which station they are in, ring us with their name and date of birth.

## What genuinely helps

1. **Instruct a solicitor straight away.** You can contact a solicitor on someone's behalf. They then confirm it, and we act for them, not for you. This is the single most useful thing you can do.
2. **Tell us about medication and health.** Epilepsy, diabetes, a heart condition, pregnancy, an injury, withdrawal. This needs to reach the member in charge quickly.
3. **Tell us about vulnerability.** A mental health condition, an intellectual disability, autism, a history of self harm, or a tendency to agree with whatever is put to them under pressure. It affects how a person should be dealt with in interview.
4. **Tell us about language.** If English is not their first language, or they would struggle in a formal interview, an interpreter can be requested.
5. **Say if they are under 18.** A child has additional protections, including notification of a parent or guardian and, in the ordinary course, an appropriate adult present during questioning.
6. **Gather practical information for bail,** quietly, before it is needed: address, employment or college details, and who might act as a surety.
7. **Look after the practicalities at home.** Childcare, an employer, a car left somewhere, a pet.

## What not to do

Families rarely make things worse deliberately. They make things worse by trying to help.

- **Do not contact the complainant, their family, or anybody you believe is a witness.** Not to apologise, not to explain, not to ask what happened, not to ask them to withdraw anything. Not by phone, message, mutual friend, or by turning up. Contact of this kind can be treated as interference with a witness. It can lead to further charges and it is one of the strongest reasons a court can be given for refusing bail. Asking somebody else to make that contact for you is the same act.
- **Do not post about it on social media,** and ask others not to. Not an appeal, not a denial, not a comment under someone else's post. Screenshots outlast deletions.
- **Do not construct an account for them.** Do not tell them what to say, agree a version of events, or "remind" them where they were. It can amount to an offence in itself, and it removes the one advantage they had, a truthful account given with proper advice.
- **Do not delete anything,** and do not move or dispose of property connected to what is alleged.
- **Do not go to the station and become angry.** It will not help the person inside and it can result in you being arrested too.

## Bail, and where family really matter

If the person is charged and not released on station bail, bail will be considered by a court. This is where a family can make a genuine, legitimate difference.

Courts look at practical things: whether there is a settled address, whether the person will turn up, and whether somebody is prepared to stand behind them. A family member who attends with documents in order is worth a great deal more than one who is upset in the corridor. What to have ready:

- **An address** where the person can live, and the agreement of whoever lives there.
- **A surety.** An independent person, often a family member, who undertakes to the court and may be required to show they have means. Bring identification and evidence of means.
- **Evidence of ties**: employment, a college course, children, caring responsibilities.
- **Anything relevant to conditions**: whether a curfew is workable, whether signing on at a station is practical, whether a passport can be surrendered.
- **Support for medical or addiction issues**: a GP, a treatment place, an appointment.

Give this to the solicitor rather than handing it to the court yourself. A surety takes on a real obligation, so ask us to explain it before you agree.

One thing to be clear about throughout: if you instruct us for your son, daughter, partner or parent, they are the client. We take instructions from them and owe them confidentiality, so there may be things we cannot pass on to you. We keep you informed to the extent our client agrees.

## Common questions

### Will the Gardai tell me why they have been arrested?

Not usually in any detail. You can be told the person is in custody, and your message about a solicitor can be passed on. The person themselves should be told what they have been arrested for, and their solicitor can find out more.

### Can I visit them or bring anything in?

Visits in custody are not ordinarily permitted. Medication is the exception worth asking about, so ring the station.

### My child is 16 and has been arrested. What is different?

A child in custody has additional protections, including that a parent or guardian is notified and, in the ordinary course, present during questioning. Do not let an interview take place before you have spoken to a solicitor.

### They already spoke to the Gardai without a solicitor. Is it too late?

No. Ring anyway. What has been said cannot be unsaid, but the matter may still be at an early stage and further interviews may follow.

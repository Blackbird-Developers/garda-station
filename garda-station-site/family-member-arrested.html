Home › A family member is in custody

The most useful thing you can do in the next ten minutes

Phone the Garda station and tell the member in charge that a solicitor is being instructed and name us. Then call 087 122 3080. A detained person is entitled to a solicitor of their choosing, and that request can be communicated.

What you can find out

You can usually establish which station the person is being held at and that they are there. Detail about the investigation itself will not generally be shared with you — that is not obstruction, it reflects the fact that the information belongs to the person detained.

Ask the station: is the person there, who is the member in charge, and has a solicitor been requested.

Practical things that genuinely help

  • Instruct a solicitor. This is by far the most valuable thing you can do, and it can be set in motion by a family member.
  • Medication and medical needs. Tell the member in charge immediately if the person has a medical condition, needs prescribed medication, or has a mental health difficulty. This matters and it is acted on.
  • Age and vulnerability. If the person is under 18, or has an intellectual disability or a condition affecting their ability to understand the process, say so at once — additional protections apply.
  • Language. If English is not their first language, an interpreter should be provided. Say so.
  • Practical items. Ask the station what you may bring, such as appropriate clothing.

What not to do

  • Do not contact anyone else involved in the matter — not the complainant, not witnesses, not anyone else who may be connected to it. However well meant, this can be interpreted very badly and can create an entirely new and serious problem, including for you.
  • Do not post about it. Social media posts are seen, screenshotted and produced later. This includes messages in group chats.
  • Do not try to piece together an account for them. Let them give their own account, with advice.
  • Do not assume it will resolve itself if everyone stays quiet. Get advice.

If they are charged

They may be released on station bail with a court date, or brought before the next sitting of the District Court where bail is decided by a judge. Family members are often relevant to a bail application — an address, a willingness to act as an independent surety, or evidence of employment or caring responsibilities can all matter. Tell us what you can offer.

You are allowed to ask for help on their behalf

You do not need to know the law and you do not need to have all the facts. Call us, tell us which station and what you have been told, and we will take it from there.

Talk to a solicitor before you talk to the Gardai.

One call, at any hour. If you qualify for legal aid it costs you nothing — and either way you will know exactly where you stand.

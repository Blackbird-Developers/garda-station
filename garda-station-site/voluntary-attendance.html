“Just come in for a chat”

An invitation to attend a Garda station voluntarily can sound like a formality or a favour. It is neither. It deserves exactly the same care as an arrest.

Why you are being asked

The Gardai take statements when they are investigating something. If they want to speak to you in a station, it is because you have been connected to a matter under investigation in some way — as a witness, as a person who may have relevant information, or as a suspect.

You will not always be told which of those you are. And it can change during the conversation.

The risk people do not see coming

We have acted for clients who attended believing they were helping as a witness, and who realised partway through that the questions had turned towards them. By that point an account has already been given, without advice, on the record. Ten minutes on the phone beforehand would have changed the entire position.

What “voluntary” actually means

It means you are not under arrest. In principle you are free to leave, and you are not obliged to attend at all. What it does not mean is that the conversation is informal, off the record, or without consequence. What you say can be recorded, relied upon, and used.

It is also not unusual for a voluntary attendance to be followed by an arrest, if the investigation moves that way.

Before you agree to attend

  1. Do not commit to a time on the spot. It is perfectly reasonable to say you will confirm once you have spoken to your solicitor.
  2. Find out what it is about. Ask what matter is being investigated and in what capacity you are being asked to attend.
  3. Call us first. We will tell you what your position is likely to be, whether you should attend, and on what basis.
  4. Do not attend alone if you are a suspect. If there is any prospect that you are, your solicitor should be with you.

Should you go at all?

Sometimes yes. There are situations in which attending, with advice and with your solicitor present, is clearly the right course — and situations where giving a clear account early genuinely helps. There are also situations where it is not in your interests at all. That assessment depends on facts we need to hear before we can give you a view.

What it costs to ask

Nothing. The initial phone call is free and confidential, and we will tell you on that call whether you are likely to qualify for legal aid.

Before you say anything to the Gardai, speak to a solicitor.

If you have been arrested, or asked to attend a Garda station to “have a chat” or give a statement, you are entitled to legal advice before you are interviewed — and you are entitled to have a solicitor present. We attend Garda stations across Dublin and nationwide, at any hour.

If the Gardai are waiting, do this now

You do not have to answer questions before you have spoken to a solicitor. Tell the member you want to speak to your own solicitor, give them our name, and call us on 087 122 3080. If you are already in the station, ask the member in charge to contact us for you — they are obliged to facilitate your request for legal advice.

What to do, step by step

Most people have never been arrested and have no idea what happens next. This is the sequence we will take you through.

  1. Say nothing about the allegation until you have legal advice. You are not being rude and you are not making yourself look guilty. You are exercising a right you have precisely because these situations are serious.
  2. Call us on 087 122 3080 — day or night. You will speak to a criminal defence solicitor, not a call centre and not an answering service.
  3. We explain what is likely to happen next. What you are suspected of, how long you can be detained, what the interview process involves, and what your options are.
  4. We agree a plan before any interview begins. Whether to answer questions, to answer some, or to say nothing is a decision that should be made with advice — not under pressure in an interview room.
  5. We attend with you. At the Garda station, at our offices, or wherever suits. If you qualify for the Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme, there is no cost to you.

Your rights in a Garda station

These rights apply whether you are innocent or not, and whether you have been arrested or asked to attend voluntarily.

The solicitor of your choice

You are entitled to consult a solicitor of your own choosing. You do not have to accept a solicitor suggested to you, and you are not obliged to stay with a solicitor who has acted for you before.

Legal advice before questioning

You are entitled to consult a solicitor before an interview begins. If you ask for a solicitor, questioning should not proceed until you have had that advice.

A solicitor present during interview

You are entitled to have your solicitor present while you are being interviewed — not just a phone call beforehand.

The right to silence

You are not obliged to answer questions. In certain limited circumstances a court may draw inferences from a failure to answer — which is exactly why the decision needs to be made with advice.

The presumption of innocence

It is for the State to prove a case. Being questioned, arrested or charged does not change that, whatever impression is created in the interview room.

To have someone notified

You are entitled to have a nominated person told where you are, and to be told your rights in a language you understand.

Common questions

The Gardai said I'm only a witness. Do I still need a solicitor?

It is worth getting advice first. People sometimes attend believing they are assisting as a witness and find during the interview that the questions have turned towards them. A short phone call before you attend costs you nothing and tells you where you stand.

Will asking for a solicitor make me look guilty?

No. Asking for legal advice is the exercise of a right, and it is not evidence of anything. Innocent people are interviewed every day, and the interview is exactly where an innocent person is most likely to be misunderstood.

Will it delay my release if I insist on waiting for a solicitor?

You may be told that it will. In practice, the period of detention is governed by the legislation under which you are held and by the investigation itself. Speak to us and we will tell you honestly what to expect in your situation.

How much will this cost?

Legal advice at a Garda station is covered by the Garda Station Legal Advice Scheme for those who qualify, and is means tested. We will assess your eligibility on the phone, before anything is agreed. If you do not qualify, we will tell you clearly what our fees are before you commit to anything. See legal aid explained.

I already have a solicitor. Can I change?

You are entitled to choose, and to change, your own legal representation at any stage. If you would like to discuss moving a file to us, call and we will explain the process.

Do you only cover Dublin?

Our five offices are across Dublin and that is where we can attend fastest. We do act for clients elsewhere in Ireland — call and we will tell you honestly whether we can get to you in time, or help you find someone who can.

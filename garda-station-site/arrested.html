You have been arrested. What happens now?

Arrest is frightening and disorienting, and most people going through it have never been in a Garda station before. Here is what the process actually looks like.

If this is happening right now

Tell the member in charge that you want to speak to your own solicitor before you are interviewed, and give them our details. Then say nothing about the allegation until we have spoken. Call 087 122 3080.

Arrival and the custody record

When you are brought to the station you will be processed by the member in charge, who is responsible for your welfare and for your rights being observed. Your details, the reason for your arrest, and the times of everything that follows are entered in the custody record. You should be told why you are being detained and informed of your rights, including your right to a solicitor.

Searches, property and samples

You may be searched and your property taken and listed. Depending on the offence, you may be asked to provide fingerprints, photographs or samples. Different rules apply to different types of sample — some require consent, some can be taken without it, and some carry consequences if refused. Ask to speak to your solicitor before agreeing to anything.

The interview

Interviews are usually recorded. There will normally be two members present, and there are limits on how long an interview can run and how many can take place. You are entitled to have your solicitor with you.

It is worth understanding what an interview is for. It is not an opportunity for the investigating members to be persuaded of your innocence. It is a stage of an investigation at which your account is recorded and tested. Denials are noted; they rarely end the process. What you say — and how it is later interpreted — can matter enormously.

“I'll just explain and clear it up”

This is the most common instinct, and it is the one that most often causes difficulty. Explaining is sometimes exactly the right strategy — but when, how much, and in what terms are decisions that need advice. An account given hurriedly under pressure, without knowing what the investigation actually consists of, can be very hard to move away from later.

How long it can last

The maximum period of detention depends on the statutory provision you are held under, and some provisions allow for extensions authorised by a senior officer or by a court. Rest periods may not count towards the total. We will tell you which regime applies to you.

How detention ends

Broadly, one of the following happens:

  • You are released without charge. That may be the end of it, or a file may still go to the Director of Public Prosecutions for a decision.
  • You are charged and released on station bail with charge sheets and a date to appear in court.
  • You are charged and brought before the next available court, where bail is dealt with by a judge.
  • You are released and re-arrested in relation to another matter, or detention is extended under the relevant provision.

What we do next

If you are charged, the case does not begin and end in the Garda station. We will advise you on bail, obtain and review the disclosure in your case, advise on plea and on the strength of the evidence, brief counsel where the case requires it, and represent you through the District, Circuit, Central or Special Criminal Court as appropriate.
